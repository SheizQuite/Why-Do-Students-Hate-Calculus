# Beyond the Numbers: What Makes Calculus Difficult for HSC Students?

## Overview

I was interested in understanding why students find Calculus difficult. To explore this question, I conducted a survey of 65 students.

My project examines students' perceptions of Calculus, their learning strategies, study habits, problem-solving challenges, and the factors that may contribute to difficulties in learning the subject.

## Research Question

**What makes Calculus difficult for HSC students?**

## Methodology

I conducted a survey using Google Forms and received a total of **65 responses**.

The questionnaire explored the following areas:

- Student demographics
- Perceived difficulty of Calculus
- Study habits and practice frequency
- Difficult Calculus topics
- Reasons Calculus feels difficult
- Learning strategies
- Learning resources
- Problem-solving challenges
- Personal experiences with Calculus

## Participants

- **Class 11 (1st Year):** 28 students (43.1%)
- **Class 12 (2nd Year):** 36 students (55.4%)
- **BSc 1st Year:** 1 student (1.5%)

Additionally, **96.9%** of respondents had chosen Higher Mathematics as a subject.

## Key Findings

### 1. Calculus Is Widely Perceived as Difficult

- **56.9%** of respondents rated Calculus as difficult or very difficult.
- Only **12.3%** rated it as easy or very easy.

### 2. Formula Overload Is a Major Concern

- **53.8%** of respondents reported that there were too many formulas and rules to remember.
- This was the most frequently selected reason for difficulty.

### 3. Memorization Is a Common Learning Strategy

- **47.7%** of students reported memorizing formulas and practicing problems.
- Only **29.2%** reported focusing on deep conceptual understanding before solving problems.

### 4. Application-Based Topics Appear Most Difficult

The most frequently selected difficult topics were:

- **Applications of Differentiation:** 41.5%
- **Definite Integration:** 38.5%
- **Differentiation Rules:** 36.9%
- **Indefinite Integration:** 36.9%

### 5. Problem-Solving Challenges Extend Beyond Formulas

Students most frequently reported difficulty with:

- **Performing calculations correctly:** 36.9%
- **Applying methods step by step:** 30.8%
- **Identifying the correct concept or rule:** 29.2%

Only **1.5%** selected remembering formulas as the most challenging part of solving a Calculus problem.

## Main Finding

One of the most interesting findings I discovered was the contrast between what students perceive as the main source of Calculus difficulty and the challenges they report when solving problems.

While **53.8%** of respondents identified the large number of formulas and rules as a reason Calculus feels difficult, only **1.5%** selected remembering formulas as the most challenging part of solving a Calculus problem.

Instead, respondents more frequently reported difficulties with performing calculations correctly (36.9%), applying methods step by step (30.8%), and identifying the appropriate Calculus concepts or rules (29.2%).

This contrast suggests that students' perceived difficulties with Calculus may extend beyond formula memorization to include the practical application of mathematical knowledge. However, since these questions examined different aspects of the learning experience, the findings reveal a notable pattern rather than establish a direct relationship between perceived formula overload and problem-solving difficulties.

## Limitations

- The survey included only 65 respondents, most of whom were Class 11 and Class 12 students from the Sylhet region.
- The findings primarily reflect the experiences of students from Sylhet who had chosen Higher Mathematics.
- The data were self-reported and did not directly measure students' mathematical abilities.
- The survey identifies patterns in students' experiences but cannot establish causal relationships.

Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as insights into this sample from Sylhet rather than as conclusions about all HSC students in Bangladesh.

## Conclusion

This survey suggests that students' difficulties with Calculus involve more than remembering formulas. Although formula overload was the most frequently reported concern, students also identified calculation accuracy, method application, and selecting appropriate concepts as important challenges.

The findings highlight the importance of examining how students apply mathematical knowledge, not just how they memorize rules. Further research with a larger and more diverse sample, combined with direct assessments of problem-solving skills, could help investigate these patterns more thoroughly.
