Participant Demographics

Q2. Did you choose Higher Mathematics as a subject in college?
Yes: 63 (96.9%)
No: 2 (3.1%)
Observation: Nearly all respondents had chosen Higher Mathematics.

Interpretation: The survey responses come mainly from students who have direct experience with Calculus.

Q3. Have you studied Calculus yet?
Completed the Calculus chapters in syllabus: 30 students (46.2%)
Currently studying Calculus: 35 students (53.8%)
Observation: I found the respondents were almost evenly divided between students who had completed Calculus and those who were still studying it.

Interpretation: This balance provides perspectives from both current learners and students who have already completed the topic, making the survey results more representative of different stages of the Calculus learning experience.

Q4. How difficult do you find Calculus?
Rating 1: 1 student (1.5%)
Rating 2: 7 students (10.8%)
Rating 3: 20 students (30.8%)
Rating 4: 26 students (40.0%)
Rating 5: 11 students (16.9%)
Observation: More than half of the respondents (56.9%) rated Calculus as difficult or very difficult (ratings 4 or 5).

Interpretation: The results suggest that Calculus is widely perceived as a challenging subject among students. Very few students considered it easy, indicating that difficulties with Calculus are common rather than isolated.

Q5. How often do you practice Calculus problems outside class?
5+ days a week: 15 students (23.1%)
3-4 days a week: 35 students (53.8%)
1-2 days a week: 11 students (16.9%)
Less than once a week: 3 students (4.6%)
Never: 1 student (1.5%)
Observation: Most students practice Calculus regularly, with over half practicing 3-4 days per week.

Interpretation: The difficulties students face in Calculus cannot be explained simply by a lack of effort. Many students actively practice the subject but still report significant challenges, suggesting that the issue may lie in conceptual understanding rather than motivation alone.

Q6. Which topic do you find most difficult?
Limits and Continuity: 10 students (15.4%)
Differentiation Rules: 24 students (36.9%)
Applications of Differentiation: 27 students (41.5%)
Indefinite Integration: 24 students (36.9%)
Definite Integration: 25 students (38.5%)
Applications of Integration (Area under a curve): 20 students (30.8%)
Observation: Applications of Differentiation was the most frequently selected difficult topic, followed closely by Definite Integration.

Interpretation: Students appear to struggle more with application-based topics than with introductory concepts such as Limits and Continuity. This suggests that applying Calculus concepts to solve complex problems may be more challenging than learning the basic rules themselves.

Q7. What is the biggest reason Calculus feels difficult to you?
My basic mathematical skills are not strong enough: 11 students (16.9%)
I struggle to understand the concepts: 16 students (24.6%)
There are too many formulas and rules to remember: 35 students (53.8%)
I do not get enough practice: 15 students (23.1%)
My textbook or teacher's explanations are difficult to follow: 17 students (26.2%)
I feel anxious when solving mathematics problems: 19 students (29.2%)
I struggle with Trigonometry and Trigonometric identities in Calculus: 15 students (23.1%)
I have not studied enough Calculus yet: 7 students (10.8%)
Observation: The most common reason students find Calculus difficult is the large number of formulas and rules they feel they must remember.

Interpretation: More than half of the respondents identified formula overload as a major challenge. In addition, mathematical anxiety and difficulty understanding explanations were also common concerns. This suggests that students struggle not only with mathematical content but also with confidence and learning approaches.

Q8. Which of the following strategies do you use to learn Calculus?
I focus on understanding concepts deeply before solving problems: 19 students (29.2%)
I memorize formulas and practice problems: 31 students (47.7%)
I rely mainly on teachers/coaching classes and notes: 23 students (35.4%)
I memorize standard rules and shortcut techniques: 12 students (18.5%)
I do not follow any specific strategy: 6 students (9.2%)
Observation: Memorizing formulas and practicing problems was the most common learning strategy among respondents.

Interpretation: Many students rely on memorization-based learning rather than deep conceptual understanding. Interestingly, the same students also identified formulas as their biggest challenge. This may suggest that an emphasis on memorization contributes to the difficulties students experience in Calculus.

Observation: Class notes, home tutors, and textbooks were the most commonly used learning resources.

Interpretation: Students rely heavily on structured learning support, particularly class materials and private tutoring. Independent learning and peer support were less common, suggesting that many students depend on guided instruction to learn Calculus.

Q10. Which part of solving a Calculus problem do you find most challenging?
Understanding what the question is asking: 16 students (24.6%)
Identifying the correct Calculus concept or rule to use: 19 students (29.2%)
Applying Algebra or Trigonometry while solving the problem: 19 students (29.2%)
Performing calculations correctly: 24 students (36.9%)
Applying the correct method step by step: 20 students (30.8%)
Remembering formulas: 1 student (1.5%)
Observation: Performing calculations correctly was the most commonly reported challenge, followed by applying methods step by step.

Interpretation: Although many students reported formula overload as a major reason Calculus feels difficult (Q7), almost no students identified remembering formulas as the hardest part of solving problems. Instead, students struggle more with applying concepts, carrying out calculations, and following solution methods. This suggests that the challenge may lie more in problem-solving and application than in memorization itself.

Key Insight

One of the most interesting findings of this survey is that 53.8% of students reported that there are too many formulas and rules to remember, making formula overload the most commonly reported challenge.
However, when asked which part of solving a Calculus problem was most difficult, only 1.5% selected remembering formulas. Instead, students reported greater difficulty with calculations, applying concepts, and following solution methods.
This suggests that students may perceive formulas as the problem, while their actual difficulties arise during problem-solving and application.

Q11. Is there anything else you would like to share about your experience learning Calculus?
Selected responses included:

"I think I didn’t learn Trigonometry before and this is why Calculus seems hard to me."
"When I study Calculus, it feels horrible like a nightmare."
"The more I practice, the easier it gets."
"I HATE CALCULUS!"

Observation: Students expressed a wide range of experiences, from frustration and anxiety to optimism about improvement through practice.

Interpretation: The open-ended responses support the patterns observed throughout the survey. Some students linked their difficulties to weak foundations in Trigonometry, while others emphasized the importance of practice. Several responses also revealed strong negative emotional reactions toward Calculus, suggesting that students' perceptions are influenced not only by academic difficulty but also by confidence and mathematical anxiety.
